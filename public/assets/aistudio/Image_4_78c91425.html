AVL Trees: Self-Balancing Invariants & Rotation Mechanics

Author: Department of Student Academic Support · Compiled for Midterm Examination Mastery

1. The AVL Balance Invariant

An AVL tree is a self-balancing binary search tree in which the heights of the two child subtrees of any node differ by at most one. For every node v in tree T:

BalanceFactor(v) = height(v.left) - height(v.right) ∈ {-1, 0, +1}

* Note: If at any time |BF(v)| ≥ 2, tree rebalancing is triggered along the insertion/deletion path via constant-time O(1) rotation operators.

2. Canonical Rotation Case Taxonomy

Case 1: Left-Left (LL) Single Right Rotation

Insertion into the left subtree of the left child of node z.

Case 2: Right-Right (RR) Single Left Rotation

Insertion into the right subtree of the right child of node z.

Case 3: Left-Right (LR) Double Rotation

Left on child Y, followed by Right on root Z.

Sequence: Left(Y) → Right(Z)

Case 4: Right-Left (RL) Double Rotation

Right on child Y, followed by Left on root Z.

Sequence: Right(Y) → Left(Z)

3. Time & Space Complexity Bounds

Operation Average Time Worst Case Space Complexity
Search / Lookup O(log n) O(log n) O(1) aux
Insertion (inc. rebalance) O(log n) O(log n) O(1) aux
Deletion (inc. cascades) O(log n) O(log n) O(1) aux
